Episode 881: Shaan Puri and Sam Parr talk to Robert Greene about living a more meaningful life.

Show Notes:

  • 0:00 People Are Suffering
    The episode begins with a discussion about Robert Greene's new book, 'Sublime,' which contrasts with his earlier works like 'The 48 Laws of Power,' 'Seduction,' and 'Mastery,' all of which focus on control and power. 'Sublime' is about embracing the awe of simply being alive, a perspective Greene gained after a near-death experience eight years ago. He argues that modern life, dominated by technology, narrows our experiences and disconnects us from the world and each other. Greene's book aims to help readers reconnect with the sublime aspects of life without needing a life-threatening event to do so, offering practical advice for integrating these experiences into daily life. He emphasizes the importance of feeling connected to oneself, others, and the environment.
  • 2:00 Everything Is Amazing and No One Is Happy
    The conversation shifts to the idea that 'everything is amazing and nobody is happy,' referencing a Louis C.K. bit. The hosts discuss how every generation believes theirs is uniquely challenging, often ignoring positive aspects like lower crime rates. Greene suggests that while technology has improved many facets of life, it also rewires our brains in unhealthy ways, contributing to a pervasive sense of powerlessness. He notes that constant interaction with technology, especially smartphones, leads to addiction and a loss of agency, as people become more disconnected from their environment and themselves. Greene emphasizes the need for situational awareness and reconnecting with the world around us to combat this modern malaise. He acknowledges uncertainty about the full impact of technology and AI, highlighting a potential crisis in how these tools shape our experiences.
  • 10:45 Go Outside
    The discussion turns to practical advice on breaking free from technology addiction. Greene suggests physically removing oneself from technology by going into nature without a phone, which forces the mind to focus outwardly and reconnect with the environment. He shares his personal experience of writing 'Sublime' while being confined due to a stroke, finding awe in everyday interactions and surroundings. Greene emphasizes the importance of slowing down, putting away technology, and engaging with the world to rediscover the sense of wonder we had as children. His book includes exercises to help readers recapture this feeling, highlighting the importance of reconnecting with one's body and environment.
  • 14:20 Feeling Flat
    The conversation delves into why adults often feel a sense of flatness despite achieving their goals. Greene explains this as an addiction to external stimulation, which leaves people empty and craving more. He argues that true fulfillment comes from internal experiences, such as love or personal achievements, rather than external validation. This internal focus leads to more meaningful and lasting satisfaction. Greene also mentions that his book 'Sublime' includes exercises to help readers reconnect with these internal experiences, emphasizing the importance of personal agency and self-driven experiences.
  • 18:00 The Immense and the Impossible
    Greene shares a compelling story from his book about a rugby team that survived a plane crash in the Andes, highlighting the human capacity to overcome impossible odds. The story illustrates the principle of 'measuring yourself against the immense and the impossible,' showing how people can discover extraordinary inner resources when faced with extreme challenges. This narrative underscores the idea that humans have untapped potential that can be unlocked in dire situations, driven by a sense of purpose and determination. Greene uses this example to demonstrate the sublime nature of human resilience and the power of facing seemingly insurmountable challenges.
  • 29:35 Say Less Than Necessary
    The conversation touches on the potential for Greene's work to be taught in educational settings, particularly 'The 48 Laws of Power'. Greene suggests that while college might not be the right venue due to its focus on theoretical knowledge, a business school could benefit from teaching his principles, which address real-world power dynamics and interpersonal politics. He highlights the practical value of understanding these dynamics in the workplace, suggesting that such knowledge could prepare students for the complexities of professional environments.
  • 30:05 Disappear
    Greene discusses the importance of strategic absence and presence in maintaining power, advising to 'say less than necessary'. He explains that constant presence can lead to being taken for granted, while strategic absence can create intrigue and maintain interest. Greene emphasizes the importance of balancing visibility with mystery, particularly in the age of social media, where oversharing is common. He cites examples of public figures who successfully manage their presence to maintain an air of mystery and keep public interest alive.
  • 35:30 Robert's AI Prediction
    Greene shares his cautious outlook on AI, expressing concerns about its potential misuse due to human nature's dark side. He predicts that public fear and resentment could lead to regulatory measures against AI. Greene is skeptical of the hype surrounding AI, noting that while it has potential benefits, it also poses significant risks, such as exacerbating societal inequalities and diminishing essential skills like critical thinking. He highlights the potential for AI to be used for harmful purposes, reflecting his pessimistic view on its unchecked development.
  • 40:55 Handwriting a 400-Page Book
    Greene describes the arduous process of handwriting 'Sublime' due to his physical limitations after a stroke. He details his method of writing and editing, which involved multiple rounds of rewriting and dictation. This labor-intensive process took seven years, underscoring Greene's dedication and commitment to his craft despite significant challenges. His experience highlights the personal sacrifices and resilience required to produce a deeply personal and meaningful work.
  • 41:25 Surviving Near-Death Experiences
    The conversation explores near-death experiences and their profound impact on individuals. Greene shares insights from his research, noting that most near-death experiences are positive, providing a sense of peace and a new perspective on life. He explains that these experiences can alter one's perception of reality, as the brain's usual constructs break down. Greene's own near-death experience has softened his fear of death, providing him with a sense of connection to something greater. This segment underscores the transformative potential of such experiences and their ability to reshape one's understanding of life and death.
  • 49:20 Mythical Leaders
    Greene reflects on the mythic qualities of great leaders, emphasizing the importance of charisma and a larger-than-life presence. He discusses how leaders like JFK and Reagan used media to project a mythic image, appealing to broader visions of America. Greene suggests that modern leaders lack this mythic quality, which he believes is essential for inspiring and uniting people. He argues that America needs leaders who embody the country's pioneering spirit and practical ingenuity, qualities that have historically defined its greatness. This discussion highlights the role of charisma and myth in effective leadership.
  • 59:10 Brimming With Confidence
    The discussion turns to the concept of charisma, with Greene explaining that many charismatic individuals come from troubled backgrounds, which fuels their need for public validation. He describes charisma as a combination of inner intensity and outer-directed attention, often driven by a deep-seated need for love and approval. Greene notes that while charisma can be taught to some extent, truly charismatic figures often possess a unique combination of personal history and inner drive. He emphasizes the importance of confidence and authenticity in developing charisma, suggesting that these qualities can be cultivated to enhance one's presence and influence.

Key Takeaways:

  • Book Insights: Robert Greene's new book, 'Sublime', focuses on experiencing awe and connecting with the world, contrasting his previous works on power and control. It offers practical exercises to help readers reconnect with their environment and themselves.
  • Technology Impact: Greene discusses the negative effects of technology, particularly smartphones, on human connection and mental health. He emphasizes the importance of disconnecting from devices to experience the world directly and regain a sense of agency.
  • Charisma Analysis: Charisma often stems from confidence and a deep connection to a cause. Greene notes that many charismatic figures have troubled backgrounds, which drive their need for public validation and attention.
  • Near-Death Experiences: Greene shares insights from his near-death experience, noting that it altered his perception of life and death. He discusses how such experiences can lead to a profound appreciation for life and a reduction in the fear of death.
  • AI Concerns: Greene expresses skepticism about AI, fearing its potential misuse due to human nature's dark side. He worries about the impact on jobs and the loss of essential skills like critical thinking and patience.
  • Leadership Qualities: Great leaders often possess a mythic quality that transcends specific policies. Greene emphasizes the importance of charisma, confidence, and a connection to a larger vision or cause in effective leadership.
  • Practical Advice: To combat phone addiction and experience awe, Greene suggests spending time in nature without a phone, engaging in physical activities, and practicing mindfulness to reconnect with the present moment.
